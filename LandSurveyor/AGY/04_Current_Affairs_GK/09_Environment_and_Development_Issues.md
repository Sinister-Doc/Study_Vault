# 09. Environment & Development Issues (India and Karnataka)

> [!IMPORTANT] Exam focus
> The notification ends with "environment ... and development issues" for Karnataka. Expect 3–5 questions: national parks/tiger reserves, environmental laws and dates, water disputes, climate/sustainability terms. Facts below are in the order they are usually tested.

---

## 1. Basic ecology terms

- **Ecosystem** = living (biotic) + non-living (abiotic) components; **food chain / food web**; **producers** (plants), consumers, decomposers; energy flows one way, matter cycles; ~10% energy transfers between trophic levels.
- **Biodiversity** = variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels; **hotspot** = rich in endemic species and threatened (India has 4: Himalaya, **Western Ghats–Sri Lanka**, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Nicobar)). **Endemic** = found only in a region. **Endangered** listing by **IUCN Red List**.
- **In-situ conservation** (in natural habitat: national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves) vs **ex-situ** (zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks).
- **Pollution:** air (SO₂, NOₓ, PM2.5, CO), water (BOD, eutrophication), soil, noise, plastic/e-waste; **Greenhouse gases:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs; **ozone layer** (stratosphere; protects from UV; **Montreal Protocol 1987**); **acid rain** (SO₂/NOₓ); **global warming**.

## 2. Key laws and institutions (India)

| Law / body | Year | Point |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act** | 1972 | schedules of protected species; national parks and sanctuaries; **Tiger Project 1973** launched from Jim Corbett |
| **Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1974 | created Pollution Control Boards (CPCB, SPCBs) |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act** | 1980 | central approval for diversion of forest land |
| **Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1981 | air quality, PCBs |
| **Environment (Protection) Act** | 1986 | umbrella law after the **Bhopal gas tragedy 1984**; **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)** |
| **Biological Diversity Act** | 2002 | National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai); **Karnataka Biodiversity Board** |
| **National Green Tribunal (NGT)** | 2010 | fast-track environmental cases (principal bench Delhi; a southern zone bench at Chennai) |
| **Forest Rights Act** | 2006 | recognises forest dwellers' rights |
| **Compensatory Afforestation (CAMPA)** | 2016 | funds afforestation |
| **EIA** (Environmental Impact Assessment) | 1994/2006 | mandatory for big projects (mines, dams) |

- **Karnataka State Pollution Control Board (KSPCB)**, **Karnataka Forest Department**, **Karnataka State Wildlife Advisory Board**, **Karnataka Lake Conservation and Development Authority (KLCDA)**.
- **Ramsar sites** (wetlands of international importance): **Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary** (near Srirangapatna, on the Cauvery) is the classic answer; a few more Karnataka wetlands have been added in recent years, so check the latest list before the exam. **Kokkare Bellur** is a village known for pelicans and painted storks.
- **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** 17 goals adopted in 2015 for 2030; India's progress tracked by **NITI Aayog SDG India Index**; Karnataka is among the better-performing states.

## 3. Karnataka's ecology

- **Forest cover:** about **20% of the geographical area** (roughly 38,000 km²; India's is about 21–22%); **Uttara Kannada** has the highest forest cover in the state; **Western Ghats (Sahyadri)** are the main forest belt (evergreen, semi-evergreen, moist deciduous); **Shola** grasslands at high elevations; dry deciduous and scrub in the plains. **Sandalwood** (Mysuru, Sandal Soap), **teak, rosewood, bamboo**.
- **National Parks (5):** **Bandipur** (Chamarajanagar; Project Tiger reserve; also **Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve**), **Nagarhole (Rajiv Gandhi NP)** (Kodagu–Mysuru; tigers/elephants), **Bannerghatta** (Bengaluru; first biological park zoo/safari), **Kudremukh** (Chikkamagaluru; Western Ghats; lion-tailed macaque; iron-ore mining once), **Anshi** (Uttara Kannada; Dandeli–Anshi Tiger Reserve).
- **Tiger reserves:** **Bandipur, Nagarhole, BRT (Biligiri Ranganatha Temple), Bhadra, Dandeli-Anshi**; Karnataka has the **2nd-largest tiger population in India** (after Madhya Pradesh) and the **largest elephant population** in the country (Project Elephant; elephant reserves: Mysore, Dandeli). Also lion-tailed macaque (Western Ghats endemic), Malabar giant squirrel, great hornbill.
- **Wildlife sanctuaries** (a few): **Ranganathittu**, **Bhadra**, **Sharavathi Valley** (lion-tailed macaque), **Cauvery**, **Shettihalli**, **Mookambika**, **Talakaveri**, **Pushpagiri**, **Brahmagiri**, **Jogimatti**.
- **Biosphere reserve:** **Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve** (first in India, 1986; spans Karnataka–Kerala–Tamil Nadu).
- **World Heritage:** **Western Ghats** (UNESCO natural site, 2012; includes Kudremukh, Pushpagiri, Talakaveri); **Hampi** and **Pattadakal** (cultural), **Hoysala temples** (2023).
- **Lakes and urban issues:** Bengaluru's lakes (once about 1,000 lakes; encroachment, sewage and froth pollution in **Bellandur** and **Varthur**); **KLCDA** and BBMP restore them; **urban flooding**, traffic congestion, solid-waste and air quality in Bengaluru.
- **Climate:** three seasons (summer, monsoon, winter); **coastal Karnataka** gets very heavy rain (Agumbe, the "Cherrapunji of the south"), **North Interior Karnataka** is semi-arid and drought-prone (**Kolar, Chitradurga, Vijayapura, Kalaburagi, Raichur**); Karnataka has the **second-largest area of dryland** after Rajasthan (as commonly stated); the Karnataka State Natural Disaster Monitoring Centre (**KSNDMC**) issues rainfall/drought data.
- **Soil and water conservation:** watershed development (**Sujala** project, World Bank-funded), **Krishi Bhagya**, farm ponds, check dams; **Jalamrita**; groundwater over-exploitation in Kolar, Chikkaballapur, Tumakuru.
- **Renewable energy:** Pavagada Solar Park; wind farms; Karnataka has a high share of renewables in installed capacity.

## 4. Water disputes and development issues (Karnataka)

- **Cauvery water dispute** (Karnataka–Tamil Nadu, with Kerala and Puducherry): Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal (**CWDT**, 1990); final award **2007**; **Supreme Court 2018** modified the shares, giving Karnataka about **284.75 TMC** (Tamil Nadu 404.25, Kerala 30, Puducherry 7); **Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA)** formed 2018. **Mekedatu** balancing reservoir proposal (Ramanagara district) is contested. Karnataka's dams: **KRS (Krishnaraja Sagar)** and **Kabini**, **Hemavati**, **Harangi**.
- **Krishna water dispute:** **Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal** (KWDT-I 1969; KWDT-II under **Brijesh Kumar**, 2010/2013) — Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra; **Almatti dam** height and the **Upper Krishna Project** issue.
- **Mahadayi (Mandovi) dispute:** Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra; **Kalasa–Banduri** nala project for drinking water to Belagavi/Dharwad/Gadag region; Mahadayi Water Disputes Tribunal (2010; award 2018).
- **Tungabhadra dam silt** and gates issues; **Yettinahole drinking-water project** (west-flowing water diverted to the arid districts of Kolar and Chikkaballapur).
- **Human–wildlife conflict** (elephants, leopards), **sand and illegal mining**, forest encroachment, **Western Ghats protection** (**Gadgil Committee 2011** vs **Kasturirangan Committee 2013** — report to make ecologically sensitive areas); **land-use conversion** and encroachment (relevant to land records: **Kharab lands, gomala/pasture land, tank beds, "Sarkari" land encroachment**).
- **Urbanisation and infrastructure:** Bengaluru metro (Namma Metro), suburban rail, tunnel road proposals; migration; housing.
- **Regional disparities:** Kalyana Karnataka and North Interior Karnataka are less developed (Nanjundappa Committee 2002; Article 371(J)).
- **Climate action:** Karnataka has a **State Action Plan on Climate Change**; India's **NAPCC (2008)**; **Paris Agreement (2015)** and India's pledge of **net-zero by 2070**, 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; **International Solar Alliance** (co-founded by India and France; HQ Gurugram); **COP-26 "Panchamrit"**.

## 5. Sustainable development and rural angle

- **Sustainable development** (Brundtland Report 1987): meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
- Rural issues: soil degradation, groundwater decline, farm distress, migration, waste; solutions: watershed management, organic farming, agroforestry, solar pumps (**PM-KUSUM**, **Saura Krishi**), **Swachh Bharat**, **Jal Jeevan**, **MGNREGA/VB-G RAM G** works for water conservation.
- **Land-related environment tasks for a surveyor:** demarcation of forest/reserved land, tank and lake boundaries, encroachment mapping, use of **drone and satellite imagery** for change detection (see [[../03_Notes/03_Modern_Surveying_Photogrammetry_and_Remote_Sensing]], [[../03_Notes/04_Modern_Surveying_GPS_GIS_and_Advanced_Positioning]]).
