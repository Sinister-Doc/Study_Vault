## 1. Magnetism

- **Natural magnets:** magnetite (lodestone, Fe₃O₄). **Artificial magnets:** made from iron, steel, cobalt, nickel or alloys (Alnico); shapes: bar, horseshoe, needle, ring.
- **Properties:** attract iron/steel; **like poles repel, unlike attract**; a freely suspended magnet rests in the **north–south** direction; poles always occur in pairs (monopoles cannot be isolated); repulsion is the sure test of magnetism; strongest at the poles.
- **Magnetic field:** region around a magnet where its force is felt. **Detection:** a compass needle deflects; iron filings arrange along field lines.
- **Field lines:** run from N to S outside the magnet (S to N inside); form closed loops; never cross; closer lines = stronger field.

| Substance | Response | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ferromagnetic** | strongly attracted, can be permanent magnets; lose it above the **Curie temperature** | iron, cobalt, nickel, steel |
| **Paramagnetic** | weakly attracted | aluminium, platinum, sodium, oxygen |
| **Diamagnetic** | weakly repelled | bismuth, copper, gold, zinc, water, silver |
| Non-magnetic | not affected | wood, plastic, glass |

- **Preparing magnets:** *mechanical* — single touch, double touch, divided touch (stroking steel with a magnet); *electrical* — place steel in a **solenoid** carrying direct current. Soft iron loses magnetism quickly (used in electromagnets); steel retains it (permanent magnets).
- **Demagnetising:** heating, hammering, alternating current.
- **Uses of magnets:** compass, motors/generators, loudspeakers, magnetic tapes/cards, MRI, separating iron.
- **Earth's magnetism:** Earth acts like a giant bar magnet with its **magnetic south pole near geographic north**; **declination** = angle between magnetic and geographic meridian; **dip** = angle a needle makes with horizontal (0° at the magnetic equator, 90° at the poles). Relevant to compass surveying.
- **Electromagnetism:** **Oersted (1820)** showed a current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field; direction by right-hand thumb rule; a **solenoid** behaves like a bar magnet; an **electromagnet** = solenoid + soft iron core (strength ↑ with current, turns, core).
- **Instruments using the magnetic effect of current:** electric bell, galvanometer/ammeter/voltmeter, electric motor, relay, loudspeaker, electromagnetic crane, telegraph.

## 2. Electricity

**Units and laws**

| Quantity | Unit | Relation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Current $I$ | ampere (A) | $I=Q/t$ |
| Potential difference $V$ | volt (V) | $V=W/Q$ |
| Resistance $R$ | ohm (Ω) | $R=V/I$ |
| Power $P$ | watt (W) | $P=VI=I^2R=V^2/R$ |
| Energy | joule / **kilowatt-hour (kWh)** | 1 kWh $=3.6\times10^6$ J = 1 "unit" |

- **Ohm's law:** $V=IR$ (at constant temperature). Resistance $R=\rho\dfrac lA$ (resistivity ρ).
- Series: $R=R_1+R_2$ (same current). Parallel: $\dfrac1R=\dfrac1{R_1}+\dfrac1{R_2}$ (same voltage, less than the smallest).
- 1 horsepower $=746$ W. Practical unit of electrical energy: kWh; bill = units × rate.

**Effects of electric current**
- **Heating:** Joule's law $H=I^2Rt$ — heater, iron, bulb, fuse.
- **Chemical:** electrolysis, electroplating, battery charging.
- **Magnetic:** electromagnet, motor.
- **Electric fuse:** thin wire of low melting-point alloy (tin–lead) placed in **series**; melts if current exceeds its rating and breaks the circuit; **always connected to the live wire**. (MCB is the modern version.)
- **Electric bulb:** **tungsten** filament (very high melting point, 3,400 °C) in an inert gas (argon/nitrogen) so it doesn't burn; glows by heating effect.

**Sources of electricity**
- **Electric (voltaic/primary) cell:** chemical energy → electrical energy. Simple cell: Zn and Cu plates in dilute H₂SO₄. Primary cells cannot be recharged; secondary (lead-acid, Li-ion) can.
- **Dry cell (Leclanché):** zinc container (negative), carbon rod with brass cap (positive), paste of **MnO₂ + NH₄Cl + carbon**; emf **1.5 V**.
- **Electromotive force (EMF):** the **energy supplied per unit charge** by a source, equal to the potential difference across its terminals when **no current** flows. Terminal voltage $V=E-Ir$ ($r$ = internal resistance).
- Other sources: generators (AC), solar cells, thermocouples.

## 3. Electromagnetic radiation

- **EM waves** are **transverse**, need **no medium**, travel at $c=3\times10^8$ m/s in vacuum: $c=f\lambda$; carried by oscillating electric and magnetic fields at right angles. Energy $E=hf$ (higher frequency = more energy).
- **Spectrum (increasing frequency / decreasing wavelength):** radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays.

| Radiation | Uses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Radio waves** | broadcasting, communication, radar |
| **Microwaves** | ovens, mobile networks, satellite links, radar |
| **Infrared** | remote controls, night vision, thermal imaging, physiotherapy, heat |
| **Light (visible, 400–700 nm; VIBGYOR)** | vision, photography, photosynthesis |
| **Ultraviolet** | sterilisation, detecting forged notes, vitamin D formation, welding (harmful to eyes/skin); ozone absorbs it |
| **X-rays** | medical imaging, airport security, crystal structure (Röntgen 1895) |
| **Gamma rays** | cancer radiotherapy, sterilising food/equipment, most penetrating |

- **Laser** = **L**ight **A**mplification by **S**timulated **E**mission of **R**adiation. **Characteristics:** monochromatic (one wavelength), coherent (in phase), highly directional (very little spreading), very intense. **Uses:** surgery (eye), cutting/welding, barcode scanners, CD/DVD, optical fibre communication, holography, **EDM/total station distance measurement, LiDAR**.

## 4. Electronics

- **Radio communication:** sound → electrical signal → **modulated** on a high-frequency **carrier wave** → transmitted → received → **demodulated** → sound.
  - **Transmitter:** microphone, oscillator (carrier), modulator, amplifier, transmitting antenna.
  - **Receiver:** receiving antenna, tuner (selects the station by resonance), **detector/demodulator**, amplifier, loudspeaker.
  - **AM** (amplitude modulation; medium/short wave; more noise) vs **FM** (frequency modulation; VHF; better quality). Sky waves reflect from the **ionosphere**.
- **Television:** camera converts image to electrical signals (scanning line by line); transmitted with sound; receiver's **picture tube/display** reconstructs the image; colour from red, green, blue (RGB). Frames per second ≈ 25 (India, PAL).
- **Basic components:** diode (one-way conduction; rectifier), transistor (amplifier/switch), resistor, capacitor, LED, IC.

## 5. Dynamics

- **Motion:** change of position with time. **Distance** (scalar, total path) vs **displacement** (vector, shortest straight-line change). **Speed** = distance/time (scalar); **velocity** = displacement/time (vector); **acceleration** $a=\dfrac{v-u}{t}$ (m/s²); retardation = negative acceleration.
- **Equations of motion** (uniform acceleration):
$$v=u+at,\qquad s=ut+\tfrac12at^2,\qquad v^2=u^2+2as$$
- **Graphs:** distance–time — slope = speed (straight line = uniform speed; horizontal = rest). Velocity–time — **slope = acceleration; area under the graph = displacement**.
- **Periodic (oscillatory) motion** repeats at regular intervals: time period $T$, frequency $f=1/T$ (Hz), amplitude.
- **Simple pendulum:** $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac lg}$. Laws: (i) **T is independent of mass and material** of the bob; (ii) independent of **amplitude** (small angles up to about 10°); (iii) $T\propto\sqrt l$; (iv) $T\propto1/\sqrt g$. **Seconds pendulum** has $T=2$ s (length about 0.994 m ≈ 1 m at Earth). Uses: clocks, measuring $g$, detecting mineral/height variations of $g$. A pendulum clock runs slow at the equator/at altitude.
- **Waves:** transverse (particles ⟂ direction: light, water ripples, string) vs longitudinal (particles ∥ direction: sound, compressions and rarefactions). $v=f\lambda$.
- **Work and energy:** work $W=F\,s\cos\theta$ (joule); power $P=W/t$. Kinetic $KE=\tfrac12mv^2$; potential $PE=mgh$. **Law of conservation of energy:** energy is neither created nor destroyed. Forms: mechanical, heat, light, sound, chemical, electrical, nuclear. Sources: **renewable** (solar, wind, hydro, biomass, tidal, geothermal) and **non-renewable** (coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear).
- **Newton's laws:**
  1. **First (inertia):** a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an external force.
  2. **Second:** $F=ma$ (force = rate of change of momentum, $p=mv$); unit newton (kg·m/s²).
  3. **Third:** to every action there is an equal and opposite reaction (on different bodies) — rocket, gun recoil, swimming.
  - Conservation of momentum: $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$.
- **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$)** $\approx9.8$ m/s² (about 9.81), same for all bodies in vacuum. **Free fall equations:** $v=gt$, $h=\tfrac12gt^2$, $v^2=2gh$ (thrown upward: $u$ positive, $g$ negative; time of ascent $=u/g$; max height $u^2/2g$).

## 6. Heat

- **Heat** = energy transferred due to temperature difference (SI unit joule; also calorie, **1 cal = 4.186 J**, kcal). **Temperature** = degree of hotness (measure of average kinetic energy). Heat flows from hot to cold. Modes: conduction, convection, radiation.
- **Scales**

| Scale | Ice point | Steam point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Celsius (°C) | 0 | 100 |
| Fahrenheit (°F) | 32 | 212 |
| Kelvin (K, absolute) | 273 (273.15) | 373 |

$$\frac{C}{5}=\frac{F-32}{9},\qquad K=C+273$$
Same reading at **−40** (°C = °F). Absolute zero $=0$ K $=-273.15$ °C.
- **Thermometers:** **mercury** (uniform expansion, opaque, shiny, doesn't wet glass, wide range −39 °C to 357 °C); **clinical** (range 35–42 °C or 94–108 °F, has a **constriction** so mercury does not fall on its own; shake to reset); **maximum–minimum (Six's)** thermometer records the day's highest and lowest temperatures.
- **Changes of state:** melting, freezing, boiling/vaporisation, condensation, sublimation; **latent heat** absorbed/released without temperature change. Water: latent heat of fusion 80 cal/g (334 J/g), vaporisation 540 cal/g (2260 J/g). Specific heat capacity of water 4.2 J/g°C (1 cal/g°C).
- **Effects of heat:** rise in temperature, change of state, **thermal expansion** (solids, liquids, gases).
- **Linear expansion:** $\Delta L=\alpha L\,\Delta T$ (α = coefficient); applications: gaps in rails and bridges, bimetallic strip in thermostats, tape corrections in surveying (steel tape). Water is anomalous: maximum density at 4 °C.

## 7. Light

**Refraction:** bending of light when it passes from one medium to another.
- **Laws:** (1) incident ray, refracted ray and the normal lie in the same plane; (2) **Snell's law:** $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\text{constant}=n$.
- **Refractive index** $n=\dfrac{c}{v}=\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ (air 1, water 1.33, glass about 1.5, diamond 2.42). Light bends **toward** the normal entering a denser medium and away when entering a rarer medium. Effects: apparent depth (a pond looks shallower), bent stick in water, twinkling of stars, advance sunrise.

**Total internal reflection (TIR):** when light travels from a **denser to a rarer** medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the **critical angle** $C$ ($\sin C=1/n$), it is reflected completely. Conditions: denser → rarer; $i>C$.
- **Applications:** optical instruments (prism periscopes, binoculars); **optical fibre cables** (core–cladding; endoscopy, telecom); **sparkling of diamond** (critical angle 24.4°); **bicycle/road reflectors** (prisms); **mirage** (hot desert road: air layers refract light upward; TIR in nature).

**Prism and dispersion:** refraction in a prism; **dispersion** = splitting white light into seven colours **VIBGYOR** (violet bends most, red least), because refractive index varies with wavelength. Natural dispersion: **rainbow** (raindrops), blue sky (scattering), red sunrise/sunset. A spectroscope analyses spectra (emission/absorption; identifies elements).

**Lenses**
- Convex (converging, thicker at centre) and concave (diverging, thinner at centre). A thin lens acts as an array of prisms.
- **Lens formula:** $\dfrac1v-\dfrac1u=\dfrac1f$; magnification $m=\dfrac vu=\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}$; **power** $P=\dfrac1f$ (dioptre, $f$ in metres; convex +, concave −).
- **Sign convention (Cartesian):** measure from the optical centre; distances along the incident light are +, against are −; upward heights +, downward −. For a real object $u<0$; convex lens $f>0$; concave $f<0$.
- **Images by convex lens:** object at infinity → real, point image at $F$; beyond $2F$ → real, inverted, diminished; at $2F$ → real, inverted, same size; between $F$ and $2F$ → real, inverted, magnified; at $F$ → image at infinity; **between $F$ and lens → virtual, erect, magnified (magnifying glass)**.
- **Concave lens:** always **virtual, erect, diminished**, between the object and the lens.
- **Defects of vision:** **Myopia (short-sightedness)** — distant objects blurred; image forms **in front of the retina**; corrected with a **concave** lens. **Hypermetropia (long-sightedness)** — near objects blurred; image **behind the retina**; corrected with a **convex** lens. Presbyopia — old age; bifocal lenses.

**Optical instruments**
- **Simple microscope:** a single convex lens of short focal length used as magnifying glass; magnification $M=1+\dfrac Df$ ($D=25$ cm).
- **Compound microscope:** two convex lenses — objective (very short $f$) and eyepiece; final image virtual, inverted, highly magnified.
- **Telescope (astronomical):** objective of large $f$/aperture, eyepiece of small $f$; **binoculars:** two telescopes with prisms to erect the image.
- **Camera:** convex lens forms a **real, inverted, diminished** image on the film/sensor; aperture controls light, shutter controls time; same as the eye (retina = film, iris = diaphragm).
- **Raman effect:** discovered by **C.V. Raman** (1928); when monochromatic light is scattered by a transparent substance, some scattered light has **changed wavelength**, giving information about molecular structure. Nobel Prize in Physics 1930; **National Science Day = 28 February**.

## 8. Sound

- Sound is a **longitudinal mechanical wave**; needs a medium (not in vacuum). Speed: air about 343 m/s at 20 °C (331 m/s at 0 °C), water about 1,480–1,500 m/s, steel about 5,000 m/s (faster in solids). Increases with temperature.
- Audible range **20 Hz–20 kHz**; **infrasonic** <20 Hz (earthquakes, elephants); **ultrasonic >20 kHz** (bats, dolphins, dog whistles).
- **Uses of ultrasonic waves:** **SONAR**, medical **ultrasound scanning** (sonography), cleaning delicate parts, detecting flaws in metals (non-destructive testing), homogenising, welding.
- **SONAR = SOund NAvigation And Ranging:** emits ultrasonic pulses; the time $t$ for the echo gives $d=\dfrac{v\,t}2$; used to find sea depth, submarines, fish shoals, shipwrecks.
- **Ultrasound scanner:** high-frequency waves reflect at tissue boundaries; the computer forms an image (safe for foetal imaging).
- **Doppler effect:** apparent change in frequency due to **relative motion** of source and observer — higher pitch when approaching, lower when receding (ambulance siren). **In light:** approaching source shifts light to **blue**, receding to **red** (redshift shows the universe expanding). **Applications:** radar speed guns, weather radar (Doppler radar), blood-flow measurement, astronomy, vehicle speed detection, moving-target radar.
- Echo needs a reflecting surface at least 17 m away (0.1 s persistence of hearing). Reverberation = repeated reflections.

## 9. Gravitation

- **Universal law:** every two masses attract with a force $F=\dfrac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$; $G=6.67\times10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg² (universal constant). Force acts along the line joining the centres; independent of the medium.
- **Gravity near the Earth:** $g=\dfrac{GM}{R^2}\approx9.8$ m/s².
- **Mass vs weight:** mass (kg) is constant everywhere; **weight $W=mg$** (newton) changes with $g$. Moon's $g$ is about **1/6** of Earth's; a 60 kg-weight person weighs about 10 kgf there.
- **Weightlessness:** the sensation of no weight in **free fall** — e.g. astronauts in orbit (gravity still acts; they and the spacecraft fall together).
- **Variation of $g$:** decreases with **altitude** (above the surface) and with **depth** (inside Earth; zero at the centre); at Earth's surface it is **maximum at the poles, minimum at the equator** (Earth's shape and rotation). $g$ changes with height as $g'=g\left(\dfrac{R}{R+h}\right)^2$.
- **Escape velocity** $v_e=\sqrt{2gR}$ = **11.2 km/s** (Earth); orbital velocity near the surface ≈ 7.9 km/s; geostationary period 24 h.
- **Importance:** keeps planets in orbit and holds the atmosphere; causes tides; makes satellites and GPS orbit possible; determines weight, free fall, dam design; used in surveying (**gravity anomalies, geoid model, plumb-line direction**). Kepler's laws describe planetary motion; Newton explained them by gravitation.
