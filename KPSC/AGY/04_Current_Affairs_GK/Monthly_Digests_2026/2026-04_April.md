## 1. Karnataka State Polity, Administration & Governance
1. **Karnataka Government's Article 131 Suit on Drought Relief**:
   - The State of Karnataka filed an original suit in the Supreme Court against the Union Government under **Article 131 of the Constitution** (*State of Karnataka v. Union of India*).
   - Sought immediate disbursement of **₹18,171 Crore from the National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF)** for 223 declared drought-hit taluks, contending that the Union's six-month delay in approving the High-Level Committee (HLC) report violated the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
   - Following Supreme Court bench intervention, the Union Government cleared ₹3,454 Crore in emergency financial relief to the state.
2. **Phase 1 Lok Sabha Polling in Karnataka (April 26, 2024)**:
   - Polling held across 14 parliamentary constituencies in Southern and Coastal Karnataka: Bengaluru Rural, Bengaluru North, Bengaluru Central, Bengaluru South, Mandya, Mysuru, Chamarajanagar, Hassan, Dakshina Kannada, Udupi-Chikmagalur, Chitradurga, Tumakuru, Kolar, and Chikkaballapura.
   - Recorded average voter turnout of ~69.5%, with Mandya recording highest turnout (>81%).
3. **Model Code of Conduct (MCC) Enforcement**:
   - Over ₹300 Crore worth of illegal cash, liquor, precious metals, and freebies seized across Karnataka by ECI flying squads and static surveillance teams.

## 6. National Polity, Constitution, Governance & Judicial Rulings
1. **Right to be Free from Climate Change Impacts (M.K. Ranjitsinh Case)**:
   - *Case*: *M.K. Ranjitsinh & Ors. v. Union of India* (3-Judge Bench led by CJI Chandrachud).
   - *Constitutional Landmark*: Held that citizens have a **fundamental right to be free from the adverse effects of climate change**, rooted in the intersection of **Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty)** and **Article 14 (Equality before the law)**.
   - Re-balanced solar overhead power line restrictions in Rajasthan and Gujarat to harmonize the conservation of the critically endangered Great Indian Bustard (*Ardeotis nigriceps*) with India's international Paris Agreement clean-energy commitments.
2. **VVPAT-EVM 100% Verification Plea Dismissed**:
   - *Case*: *Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India*.
   - *Ruling*: Supreme Court unanimously rejected pleas for 100% physical cross-verification of Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) counts with Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) slips.
   - Affirmed the credibility and tamper-proof architecture of EVMs; upheld existing protocol of verifying VVPAT slips in 5 randomly selected polling stations per assembly constituency.
   - Directed two administrative safeguards:
     1. Symbol Loading Units (SLUs) to be sealed and secured in strongrooms for 45 days post-election.
     2. Candidates securing 2nd and 3rd positions allowed to request technical verification of burnt memory of EVM microcontrollers within 7 days of results upon depositing fee.

## 16. Prelims Traps & Mains Answer Frameworks

### 16.1 Prelims Traps
> [!caution]
> - **Trap 1**: Believing the right to be free from climate change is an explicitly enumerated fundamental right in Part III.
>   - *Fact*: It is NOT explicitly enumerated; the Supreme Court in *M.K. Ranjitsinh (2024)* **judicially interpreted and derived it from Article 21 (Life) and Article 14 (Equality)**.
> - **Trap 2**: Confusing India's rank in SIPRI global military expenditure.
>   - *Fact*: India is the **4th largest military spender** ($83.6B), behind the US, China, and Russia. India is NOT 2nd or 3rd.
> - **Trap 3**: Believing the Supreme Court mandated 100% VVPAT slip verification.
>   - *Fact*: The Supreme Court **REJECTED** 100% counting and upheld the current sample of **5 polling stations per assembly constituency**.
> - **Trap 4**: Misattributing the winner of the FIDE Candidates 2024.
>   - *Fact*: **D. Gukesh** won the Candidates Tournament, becoming the youngest challenger in history; R. Praggnanandhaa also participated but did not win.

### 16.2 Mains Question & Model Blueprint
**Question**: *In the M.K. Ranjitsinh verdict, the Supreme Court recognized the Right to be Free from Adverse Effects of Climate Change. Discuss its jurisprudential basis and the challenge of balancing renewable energy expansion with ecological biodiversity conservation.* (Marks: 15 / 250 Words)

**Model Answer Outline**:
1. **Introduction (35 words)**:
   - Highlight the Supreme Court's verdict in *M.K. Ranjitsinh v. UOI (2024)* as a landmark constitutional breakthrough expanding green jurisprudence by elevating climate resilience to a non-negotiable fundamental human right under Articles 21 and 14.
2. **Body 1: Jurisprudential Foundations (70 words)**:
   - *Substantive Expansion of Article 21*: The Right to Life is rendered illusory if rising global temperatures, extreme heatwaves, unseasonal flooding, and air pollution erode the biological foundations of human existence.
   - *Article 14 Intersection*: Climate change disproportionately penalizes vulnerable agrarian populations, marginal farmers, and indigenous communities who contribute least to emissions, violating environmental equality.
   - *International Law Harmonization*: Bridges domestic constitutional guarantees with India's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the UNFCCC Paris Agreement.
3. **Body 2: The Green-vs-Green Conflict & Policy Resolution (75 words)**:
   - *The Dilemma*: Massive solar infrastructure (Pavagada, Thar deserts) requires overhead high-tension transmission wires that cause fatal collisions with the critically endangered Great Indian Bustard (*Ardeotis nigriceps* - fewer than 150 individuals surviving).
   - *Equitable Balance*: Supreme Court recalled blanket undergrounding orders across 99,000 sq km due to technical and economic non-feasibility; constituted an expert committee to demarcate core habitats where underground cabling is mandatory, while permitting bird-diverters in peripheral areas.
4. **Conclusion (25 words)**:
   - Conclude that sustainable development requires an integrated landscape-level environmental impact approach where clean-energy transitions do not cannibalize vulnerable ecological keystones.
